# What's new on the Atlas

Newest first. Generated from the working repository on every update.

## 2026-11-28 · v1.65.2: Map checked in full; hover and selection fixed

- Hovering over a service, bus stop, route, station, zone, estate or redevelopment site now shows its own details; before, the suburb's details covered them.
- Shift-click on the map adds another suburb or ward, and after selecting an area you can click another one straight away.
- Centre and station names show even with the basemap off.

## 2026-11-28 · v1.65.0: Selecting an area filters the whole map

- Choose a suburb or ward and every layer shows only what is in it: centres, services, bus stops and routes, train stations, planning zones, estates, redevelopment sites and walking catchments. Other areas fade to a faint outline and the map zooms to your selection.
- One basemap, a street map like Google Maps, replaces the grey and colour streets and the terrain shading.

## 2026-11-27 · v1.64.0: A colour street map

- Map: Layers → Background → Colour streets gives a basemap like Google Maps, with roads, parks, water and place names in colour. Grey streets is still there for a quieter background.
- Satellite view and Share this view have been removed.

## 2026-11-27 · v1.63.0: Clearer selection; one place at a time

- When you select a suburb or ward, everything outside it is veiled and the selected area is outlined and lightly tinted, so it stands out on the map.
- Choosing a centre now replaces a selected suburb or ward, and the reverse, so the rest of the page shows what belongs to the place you chose. Before, both applied at once and the Explorer was often empty.

## 2026-11-27 · v1.62.1: Clearer boundaries and service symbols; satellite link fixed

- Suburb and ward boundaries are solid lines on a light edge, so they stand out over any colour, and a selected suburb or ward has a thick outline.
- Services have a shape and colour for each group (for example a green cross for health and a blue square for education), chosen to be readable with colour-blindness.
- Satellite view ↗ opens Google Maps in satellite view more reliably.

## 2026-11-27 · v1.62.0: Services near each centre

- Map: a new Services layer shows schools, maternal and child health, kindergartens, child care, GPs, pharmacies, hospitals, aged care, libraries, community centres, places of worship, playgrounds, tennis and netball courts, sports fields, pools, gyms, supermarkets, cafés and other shops: 5,774 in all. Choose a group in the legend, zoom in to see them, and hover for the name.
- Click a centre to see Services within an 800 m walk: how many of each kind lie in the area you can reach on foot, the nearest one, and the median for all centres.
- Sources: City of Casey open data, Vicmap Features of Interest and Overture Maps places. Listings of private businesses are incomplete and some may have closed.

## 2026-11-26 · v1.61.0: Public transport network on the map

- Map: two new layers. Bus stops shows every bus stop in Casey, coloured by how many buses an hour stop there on a weekday (zoom in to see them). Train lines and stations shows the Cranbourne and Pakenham lines and their stations, with trains an hour. Hover for details; stations can be found in the map search.
- The Casey panel adds Public transport network. 82% of residents live within 400 m of a bus stop, but only 19% within 400 m of a stop with 4 or more buses an hour (about every 15 minutes), and 5% within 800 m of a train station. Distances are straight lines, so real walks are longer.
- Bus routes now include every route with a stop in Casey, not only those serving a centre.

## 2026-11-26 · v1.60.0: See it from above

- Satellite view ↗, on the map and in each centre, suburb and ward profile, opens a satellite view of the same place in a new tab (Google Maps), so you can see what is built today next to what the Atlas shows.

## 2026-11-26 · v1.59.0: Search the map; easier layers and scrolling

- Search: type part of a centre, suburb, ward or bus route number in the box at the top left of the map, then pick a result to go straight to it.
- Layers are grouped (centres, walking and transport, land and housing, community, background), each with a short description, and one button turns them all off or on.
- Scrolling the page over the map no longer zooms it by accident. Hold Ctrl (⌘ on a Mac) to zoom with the wheel, or use two fingers on a phone. In the enlarged map the wheel zooms as before.
- Share this view copies a link that opens the map with the same selection and layers. A centre's profile has links to jump to public transport, walking, zoning and consultations. On phones the map starts with more room.

## 2026-11-26 · v1.58.0: Faster first load

- The Atlas opens faster: the detailed map layers (planning zones, walking catchments, bus routes and housing data) now load only when you scroll to the map. The first download is about a third of its previous size. Everything else, including filtering by suburb or centre, works before the map loads.

## 2026-11-26 · v1.57.1: Map zoom checked and fixed

- Selecting suburbs or wards zooms the map to fit them, whether one or several. Now, a centre clicked inside a selection zooms to the centre, and clearing it returns to the selected areas. Hiding the controls or panel, or enlarging the map, zooms again to use the new space, and clearing a selection moves smoothly back to all of Casey.

## 2026-11-26 · v1.57.0: Hide the map's controls and panel

- Map: Hide controls and Hide panel, in the top-left corner of the map, put away the toolbar and the profile panel so the map has more room. Press Show controls or Show panel to bring them back. They work in the enlarged map too.

## 2026-11-26 · v1.56.0: Map opens with all layers off

- Explore the map yourself now opens with every layer switched off, including centres and the basemap, so you start from the Casey boundary and suburb colours and add only what you need from Layers.

## 2026-11-26 · v1.55.0: Walking access figures explained

- Click a centre: each walking access figure now says what it means for that centre. It gives how many of the people living within 800 m or 1.5 km in a straight line can actually walk there (for example about 4 in 10 at Berwick), homes a hectare, block size as an equivalent square block, and how much street frontage is within reach, each with the centre's rank or the median for all centres.
- A short note, How to read these together, explains how the walking-distance figures and the street-pattern figures relate.

## 2026-11-25 · v1.54.0: Bus routes on the map; bus figures explained

- Click a centre to see its bus routes on the map. The most frequent route is drawn in bold with its number, for example Route 828 at Berwick, every 20 minutes towards Hampton Station. A new layer, Bus routes, shows every route that serves a centre, thicker where buses run more often; hover for the route and its destination.
- The public transport panel says what each figure counts. "Buses on a weekday, 7 am to 7 pm" is the number of bus trips that stop within 400 m of the centre in those 12 hours, on all routes and in both directions, each bus counted once. The evening (7 to 11 pm) and Saturday figures are the same count over their hours, with the hourly average beside each.
- The figures now use the timetable for Wednesday 2 and Saturday 5 December 2026, because the earlier timetable release has been replaced. They are the same as before for every centre.

## 2026-11-25 · v1.53.0: Enlarge the map

- Map: press Enlarge map to fill the window with the map, its controls and the profile panel. Every filter and layer works as before, and the filter bar stays on top. Compare A and B shows along the bottom. Press Esc or Exit full screen to go back.
- On a phone, the enlarged map takes two-thirds of the screen, with the profile below it, and the Layers list is available.

## 2026-11-25 · v1.52.0: Planning zones on the map

- Map: a new layer, Planning zones, shows the Casey Planning Scheme zones from Vicmap Planning, coloured in 13 groups such as activity centre, residential growth, general residential and urban growth. Hover over an area for its zone code.
- Click a centre to see Zoning around the centre: how much of the land within an 800 m walk is in each zone group, next to the 800 m circle. It also shows the share in the zones meant for more homes and activity (activity centre, mixed use and residential growth). 28 of the 81 centres have any of these zones within an 800 m walk.
- Zones show what the planning scheme allows, not what is built. In growth areas the precinct structure plan, not the zone, sets the future uses, and overlays are not shown.

## 2026-11-25 · v1.51.3: Walking catchment figures: Fountain Gate corrected

- The walking figures are now on the map for all 81 centres. In the first run, Fountain Gate–Narre Warren's street frontage within reach (interface catchment) was wrongly 98 m: the street nearest the centre is a car-park road on private land, and the calculation could not leave it. It now starts on the nearest public road, 80 m away, and gives 2,754 m within 400 m and 11,558 m within 800 m. No other centre was affected.
- For the median centre, the number of residents within an actual 800 m walk is 37% of the number within the 800 m circle.
- Walking catchments are now drawn in yellow (amber in the light theme) with a firmer outline, so they stand out over any measure on the map.

## 2026-11-25 · v1.51.2: Evidence graph: Focus on… menu works again

- The Focus on… menu above the evidence graph showed as an empty box after the focus note was added. It lists every theme, policy document and consultation again.

## 2026-11-25 · v1.51.1: Map notes cover the walking catchments

- The map's notes (How to read it, and What to be careful of) now describe the walking catchment layers and their limits: each walk starts at the street nearest the centre's point, so access to large centres is understated; footpath gaps, fences and unsafe crossings are not modelled; and parks, creeks and rail land count as blocks, so a large reserve raises AwaP. They no longer say the catchments are missing from the map.

## 2026-11-25 · v1.51.0: Walking catchments for every centre

- Map: click a centre to see the area reachable on foot within 800 m and 1.5 km along streets and paths, next to the straight-line circles. A new layer shows every centre's 800 m walking catchment at once.
- The centre panel adds Walking access. It shows residents and dwellings within each walk compared with the circle, block size (AwaP, area-weighted average perimeter) and the street frontage within reach (interface catchment), with the median for all centres. The two measures come from University of Melbourne research (Pafka and Dovey, 2017) and its open-source tools.
- The figures are calculated by a separate run in GitHub and appear on the map once that run finishes.

## 2026-11-25 · v1.50.0: Map story and boundaries made clearer; walking figures corrected

- Walking distance: the story said 26% of residents live within an 800 m walk of a centre, but that counted the 32 proposed centres as if they existed. Today, with the 43 existing daily-needs centres, it is 17% within 800 m and 57% within 1.5 km; with all 81 centres built it would be 26% and 76%. The Walk access section now leads with today's figures too.
- Proposed centres now have a dashed outline with a light fill, the usual planning convention, instead of a thin hollow circle.
- The map story names the places each chapter is about (for example Clyde North – South +48,000), and each legend says what it measures. The Casey boundary is drawn on top of the roads, so it no longer disappears along the western edge.
- Suburb and ward boundaries are clearer: suburb lines are dark in the light theme (they were white) and light in the dark theme, and ward lines are thicker with a light edge.
- The story text was rewritten in plainer language.

## 2026-11-25 · v1.49.0: Evidence graph: clearer focus

- When you focus on a document, theme or consultation (by clicking it, Shift-clicking several, or choosing it in Focus on…), the graph now zooms to it. It and its direct links stay bright and the rest of the graph fades to a faint background. A note under the graph names what is in focus and how to clear it.
- The whole graph stays on screen on purpose, so you can see where the selection sits in the network. The rest of the page filters to the selection as before.

## 2026-11-25 · v1.48.0: Bus service at each centre, explained

- Map: "Centres: by bus service" now uses five clear classes, from no bus stop within 400 m to 16 or more buses an hour, with the number of centres in each. The old shading was hard to see, especially in the dark theme.
- Clicking a centre shows its public transport in full: a service level (frequent, regular, infrequent or none), the bus routes by number, weekday buses from 7 am to 7 pm, evening and Saturday buses, how often the most frequent route runs, its rank among all centres and the median for centres of the same type, and the nearest station with its distance and trains an hour. A note explains how each figure is measured.

## 2026-11-24 · v1.47.0: Map colours that work with the centre symbols

- Measures that used orange, red or pink colour scales now use blue, green or purple ones, so the red-orange activity centre symbols stay easy to see on every measure.

## 2026-11-24 · v1.46.0: Filters checked across every section

- Every filter was tested against every section in both editions: search, classification, folder, topic, level, issuer, engagement type and year, engagement theme, graph node, centre, area and leave-out. Each narrows the Explorer, the evidence graph, the charts and the engagement timeline, and clearing the filters returns the page exactly to its starting state.
- The engagement heatmap (earlier against later) now recalculates for the consultations left by your filters, and says so. With nothing filtered it gives exactly the saved results. The pattern labels (recurring, emerging, fading) still describe all consultations.
- Used in can now show "Policy and engagement analysis" for an item that is part of both.

## 2026-11-24 · v1.45.3: Background reading accepted

- The 9 shortlisted studies were accepted by the owner as background reading and now appear under New research in the technical report. They inform the method and the meaning of "community and place"; they are not policy documents and do not enter the policy analysis.

## 2026-11-24 · v1.45.2: Literature candidates screened

- The 43 studies waiting from the weekly literature scan were screened. 34 were off-topic: in other countries "activity centre" can mean a learning or children's centre, and other studies were outside planning. 9 are shortlisted as background reading for the owner to confirm. None of them is a policy document, so none would join the policy analysis.
- The scan no longer searches the US spelling "activity centers", which found none of the relevant studies.

## 2026-11-24 · v1.45.1: Technical report checked

- The technical report was read in full and corrected. Several statements no longer matched the results: Country and the centre hierarchy share 3 pages (about 23 expected by chance), not none; Country's ties are place identity and engagement, not place identity alone; and inclusion is described by its ties rather than as a main bridge. These statements are now taken from the results on every run, so they cannot drift again.
- The report no longer names a person. It says how it was prepared and that the coding has not yet been checked by a second person.
- The New research list shows only studies that have been reviewed and accepted. Until now it showed every unscreened result from the weekly scan, most of them off-topic.

## 2026-11-24 · v1.45.0: Clearer map symbols, official boundary, simpler navigation

- Activity centre symbols follow the centre hierarchy. Size and shade show rank, from Metropolitan (largest and darkest red) to Local Neighbourhood (smallest and lightest). Restricted retail precincts are grey squares and the health and education precinct is a green triangle. Proposed centres are hollow.
- The Casey boundary now comes from Vicmap, the State's official boundary. The old line had small notches along the western edge. Suburb and ward colours are trimmed to the new line, and the line has a light edge so it stands out on both themes.
- Section numbers are gone from the navigation and headings.
- Evidence graph: hold Shift and click to select several nodes. The page then shows items linked to any of them.
- Explorer: the first column is now "Used in". It says whether each item is in the policy analysis, the community engagement analysis, or kept for reference, and its button names that analysis, for example "Leave out of policy analysis".
- Wording checked throughout. Out-of-date counts were corrected, and the public edition no longer describes .id data it does not show.

## 2026-11-23 · v1.44.0: Theme links recalculate; what the items are

- Evidence graph: when policy documents are left out or filtered away, the lines between policy themes are worked out again from the documents still shown, using the same test as the analysis. A note under the graph gives the new count. Without Casey's 10 documents, for example, the link between public transport and economy drops out, and two links appear: walking with community facilities, and public transport with implementation.
- Overview: a table of what the items are (policy documents, engagement reports, structure and contribution plans, other Council documents, supporting documents, maps and data, papers and project files) and how each kind is used. The Councils figure now reads "Councils that issued items" and lists them.

## 2026-11-23 · v1.43.0: Leave items out of every section

- Explorer: each row has a Leave out button. A left-out item drops out of the evidence graph, the charts and lists, and the map's counts. It stays in the Explorer, greyed, with a button to bring it back, and the filter bar shows how many are left out.
- At this stage the links between themes were not recalculated; that came in the next update.

## 2026-11-23 · v1.42.0: Housing supply over time; a What's new log

- Urban Development Program history for Casey, from the 2017, 2018, 2022 and 2025 editions. Future estate land not yet subdivided fell from 56,606 lots in 2017 to 29,949 in 2022, then rose to 37,931 in 2025. Each edition classifies land a little differently, so treat this as a rough trend.
- It appears in the Casey panel beside the map (a table by year), in each suburb's profile and in the map story chapter "Where new homes are going".
- This What's new section, so anyone can see what changed and when.

## 2026-11-23 · v1.41.0: Compare two places: A and B circles

- Map: press Compare A and B, then drag the two pins or put them on a centre. Each circle has a radius of 300 m to 2 km.
- Each circle shows residents and dwellings (ABS mesh blocks), an age profile (ABS SA1 areas), suburb Census measures weighted by residents, Victoria in Future growth to 2036, Urban Development Program lots and redevelopment dwellings, and the centres inside it.
- The circle measures straight-line distance, not a walk along streets.
